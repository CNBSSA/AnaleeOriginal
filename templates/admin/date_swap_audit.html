Statements to check for transposed dates

Until 20 September 2026 the bank-statement importer read every cell with day-first parsing, while the file reader handed it every cell as text. A date written 2025-04-03 — or an Excel date cell, which arrives as 2025-04-03 00:00:00 — was therefore read as year-day-month and stored as 4 March instead of 3 April. Statements written 03/04/2025 were unaffected. The parser is fixed.

What this page can and cannot do. It cannot repair anything: an uploaded statement is deleted once imported and only its filename is kept, so there is nothing left to re-read. An affected statement has to be uploaded again. What this does is tell you which ones are worth opening.

How a statement gets on this list. Transposing a date moves the statement's month into the day, so every affected row lands on the same day number while its month runs 1–12. Rows dated the 13th or later are never affected, because month 13 does not exist and the parser fell back and read them correctly. A statement is listed when its rows span {{ min_months }} or more calendar months and {{ min_share }}% or more of them share one day of the month — a shape real trading does not produce.

This is a suspicion with its evidence attached, not a verdict. Open the original and compare before re-uploading anything.
